Screw Puzzle tips: holes and supports

A free hole is useful only while you can reach it. Learn to anticipate a plate’s swing, keep a destination open and compare two attempts on the same layout.

Try it on the board →
In this guide
  1. Trace the next two destinations
  2. Predict which end will stay fixed
  3. Separate access moves from release moves
  4. Use the deep board in small steps
  5. Save Undo for an observable mistake
  6. Run a repeatable opening experiment

1. Trace the next two destinations

Before moving a support, choose a specific plate and locate both of its endpoints. Identify the empty hole that will receive the first screw. Then ask where the remaining screw could go after the plate moves. Your first move creates an empty source hole, but that hole may be covered by the new plate position. An empty-hole count alone does not tell you whether the second move is possible.

2. Predict which end will stay fixed

The screw left in place is the pivot. In the illustration, moving the right screw leaves the left one holding the plate. Imagine the free end sweeping downward before you commit. Another layer may become accessible, while a hole you wanted may disappear behind the plate. The example shows a clear swing; the board edge or other constraints can stop the actual plate earlier.

3. Separate access moves from release moves

Suppose you have an exposed parked screw beside an empty hole. Moving it may reposition your working space without releasing a plate. That can be useful when the exact source hole is needed next. It is wasted motion if you simply shuttle the screw back and forth without changing access. Name the endpoint you want to reach before making a move that does not release anything.

4. Use the deep board in small steps

The current second level starts with 48 plates and three empty mounting holes. The initial screws use independent fixed points, so do not expect removing one screw to release several unrelated plates. Inspect one exposed component and its destination at a time. After a swing or drop, reassess the visible holes instead of continuing a sequence based on the old picture. On a narrow screen, pan across the mechanism before committing.

5. Save Undo for an observable mistake

A useful reason to Undo is that a swing covered the destination you had planned to use. The restored board lets you compare another destination immediately. A low progress percentage alone is not evidence that the last move was wrong. With only one Undo in Level 2, retrying the same layout can be a better way to test an opening than spending your recovery on a harmless setup move.

6. Run a repeatable opening experiment

Play until the first plate drops and note which screw you moved first and which destination you chose. Retry the same layout, change that destination, and compare the exposed holes after the swing. Keep the experiment to one changed choice. A hint search that finds no route within its limit does not establish that your position is impossible. These exercises teach access planning; they are not a universal solution to every seed.

Return to the board and test one decision on the same layout before changing the seed. Compare what becomes accessible, not just the move count.

Common questions

Why did a plate cover the hole I planned to use?+

Removing a support can rotate a plate around its remaining screw. Check the area it will swing through before choosing a parking hole. A hole that is empty can still be covered.

Must I remove the parked screws to win?+

No. The goal is to release every plate. Screws remain on the mounting board, and moving a parked screw can be useful when you need its hole for a later step.

Is a missing Hint proof that I am stuck?+

No. Hint searches within a time and search budget. It only highlights a move when it finds a complete continuation. Retry the same seed to compare an alternative opening if no continuation is found.
