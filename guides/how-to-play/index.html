How to play Screw Games: the original three modes

Start with one useful move: relocate a support, sort a top nut, or complete a color group. These illustrated examples explain what changes after each action.

1. Puzzle: select a screw, then a hole

Open Screw Puzzle at Level 1. Select an exposed screw on a plate and look for a highlighted empty destination. Select that hole to transfer the screw. If the plate still has one support, it can swing around that support; after its last support is removed, it drops. In the example below, the moved screw remains in its new hole. A screw on the bare board has not been deleted or collected.

2. Sort: uncover the nut you need

Open Screw Sort and inspect the top of each rod. A gold nut beneath a blue one cannot move yet. First transfer the blue nut onto another blue top with room, or onto an empty rod. Then select the source again to move gold. The illustration uses letters as well as colors. Each click sequence transfers one top nut, not the whole group.

Practice this move →

3. Jam: count before you collect

Open Screw Jam and inspect the buffer before choosing an exposed screw. Three of one color clear together. In the five-slot Level 2 example below, two reds and two blues leave only one slot. A third red makes room; a new color does not. Count the occupied slots after the match, not just before the tap.

4. If nothing happens when you select a piece

In Puzzle, a covered screw or occupied destination is unavailable. In Sort, the destination must have room and either be empty or match the source top. In Jam, the screw head must be exposed. In Puzzle and Sort, select the same source again to cancel the selection. Jam collects an exposed screw immediately. Use Tab to focus available controls and Enter or Space to activate them; a selection alone is not necessarily a completed move.

5. Choose the right recovery control

Undo reverses a move while keeping the cumulative move count. All current modes allow one Undo and one successful Hint per run. Level 2 adds one Rescue that creates temporary space. Practice use does not consume purchased inventory. A Hint is only shown when a complete route is found, so a timed-out search does not prove a dead end. Retry resets the same layout. New layout changes the seed and may select a familiar structure from the finite library.

6. Finish the warm-up, then test one idea

Puzzle ends when all plates are released, even though screws remain on the board. Sort ends with full single-color rods and empty spare rods. Jam ends when all screws have been collected and matched. Try Level 2 after you can explain one move in your chosen mode. Share layout preserves the starting challenge; it does not share your current position or certify a score.

Return to the board and test one decision on the same layout before changing the seed. Compare what becomes accessible, not just the move count.

Common questions

Why does tapping a screw sometimes do nothing?+

Puzzle needs two taps: select an exposed screw, then an accessible empty hole. Sort needs a source rod and a valid destination. Jam collects an exposed screw immediately. A covered screw cannot be selected.

Does running out of helpers end my attempt?+

No. You can keep making legal moves. Each current run offers one Hint and one Undo; Level 2 also allows one Rescue. Retry starts the same layout again, and does not require Points.

Will a shared link continue my current position?+

No. It opens the starting layout with the same version and seed. Use Continue on the same browser to restore your saved move sequence instead.
